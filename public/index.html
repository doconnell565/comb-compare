Load a MIDI file and hear it the way a 30, 50, or 72 tooth comb would play it. Each tooth is one fixed pitch, so a note the comb lacks is either folded up or down an octave or dropped. A tooth also cannot be plucked again until it stops ringing, so fast repeated notes fall away on the smaller combs. Cut the song to one cylinder rotation and export a pin map for machining.

What is modeled

The 30 tooth scale is the real one used by Sankyo and the paper strip boxes: two bass teeth at F3 and G3, a diatonic octave from C4, then chromatic up to F6 with G6 and A6 on top. Sankyo does not publish tuning charts for its 50 and 72 tooth movements. Those combs are tuned to each tune program rather than to a chromatic scale, and a 72 tooth comb spends many of its teeth on duplicates so bass and melody notes can repeat quickly. The 50 and 72 layouts here are approximations built to those descriptions. Once you have a movement in hand, strike each tooth, read the pitches, and enter them under Measured.
